The rule for the whole product

Where a wrong word costs money, use the exact one. Everywhere else, use the plain one. Inside a report the vocabulary stays precise, because that is what makes it usable as evidence: the four states keep their names, an article number is an article number, and provenance is stated. A pricing page is not evidence. Its only job is to let someone work out which row is theirs and what they will be charged, without asking us.

The three regions

a region is a rulebook, and you get all of it

You cannot buy Texas on its own, and you should not be able to. A state is not a rulebook — it is one edition of a national one, and a brand shipping into the US has obligations in every state it sells to whether it has thought about them or not. So a region is sold whole: the rulebook, every country or state inside it, and every official language a label is printed in there.

Two consequences of the €999 floor, for the record

The rates in the table above are our internal guide; the page says only on quotation, which is right — we are selling days of a named person's time and the scope decides the number. But at €999 the software carries a much smaller share of the contract than it did at €2,750, so the specialist capacity has to be there before we sell it, and every quote now has to earn the services revenue rather than lean on the platform fee. And with people free and recipes unlimited, the only remaining dial on this tier is expert time — which makes Advanced growth a hiring plan more than a pricing one.

Yes — "from €999" is a floor, and the quote scales with the size of the range. It should not scale by feel, though, or two customers of the same size get two different numbers and the first one to compare notes is a problem. One marginal rate, published internally, does the work: €7.50 per recipe per month above the first hundred. It is set to hold the software margin near 78% at any size, which is where the rest of the ladder sits.

Expert work is quoted on top of whichever band applies, and it is where most of the contract value sits. The alternative is a flat €999 at any size, with the people revenue carrying the margin — a legitimate land-cheap strategy, and the one to choose if we would rather compete on the software price and sell days afterwards. I would not: at three hundred recipes a flat €999 is a forty-per-cent software margin, and a customer that large is not buying on price.

Words that are never simplified

The four states keep their exact names everywhere, including on the pricing page: Clear, Action needed, Not assessed, Blocked. Not assessed in particular is the one word we must never soften — the whole position rests on saying plainly what we did not check. The same applies to the boundary sentences: we do not file, pay, correspond with an authority, confirm a registration is valid or guarantee acceptance. Those are said in full, in the plainest possible language, and never abbreviated to fit a card.

06

Why a whole region is affordable

internal · not on any page

The load-bearing fact

A check costs what the customer's own artwork costs, not what our coverage costs. A brand with a German pack and a French pack is two language panels to read whether we hold three member states or twenty-seven. The part that scales with our breadth — registration obligations, authorities, fees, deadlines, state by state — is a lookup against a register we maintain anyway, and it is close to free at check time. Selling all fifty US states therefore adds obligations to the answer, not passes to the compute bill.

Holding fifty states current is a real cost, but it is fixed and shared: every US customer pays into the same encoded ruleset. That is precisely why we are an eighty-five-per-cent-margin business and not a fifty-two-per-cent one — and it is also why the earlier per-market price list was solving a problem we do not have.

Free, Basic and Standard sit well above the 60-79% band for AI-enabled software and far above the 52% category average. Advanced at the €999 floor sits at the very top of that band rather than above it, and it is the one tier where recipe count can erode the software margin — worth a soft internal ceiling. Scoped re-checks matter less than they did under per-market pricing, because language count is now the driver rather than market count, but they still take Standard from 77% to 93%, so the engineering commitment stands.

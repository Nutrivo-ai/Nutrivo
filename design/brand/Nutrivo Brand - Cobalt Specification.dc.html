Cobalt

The chosen identity, direction 2d. Near-black type on a white ground with one bright blue used strictly for interaction. It reads as current product software rather than as a document, which is what the marketing site and the fundraise need, while the four-state verdict vocabulary keeps the evidence register intact inside the product.

This document is the single source of truth for the rollout. Where it disagrees with an existing screen, this document is correct and the screen changes.

Cobalt carries interaction only — buttons, links, focus rings, selected states, active navigation. It is never used for a verdict, a status, a chart series or decoration. On white it measures 5.0:1, so it is safe for link text as well as chrome.

Ramp roles: 100–200 tinted fills, 300 borders, 400 disabled and placeholder, 500 base, 600 hover, 700 pressed and text-on-tint, 800–900 headings and reversed grounds. Take values from the ramp rather than mixing new ones.

2 · Reserved verdict vocabulary

Four states, no binary, no fifth state. Each pairs a colour with a distinct glyph so state is never encoded by colour alone. These colours appear nowhere else in the interface. One value per state serves both the glyph and its label, chosen to clear 4.5:1 against white and against its own background tint — so a state chip is legible wherever it sits.

Clear #0F7A4D · filled square One value for glyph and label. 5.4:1 on white, 4.7:1 on its tint.
Action needed #9E5600 · triangle One value for glyph and label. 5.5:1 on white, 4.9:1 on its tint.
Not assessed #5F6570 · hollow circle One value for glyph and label. 6.4:1 on white, 5.3:1 on its tint.
Blocked #B3251F · barred square One value for glyph and label. 6.1:1 on white, 5.6:1 on its tint. A white bar is knocked out of the square, so Blocked never relies on hue to separate from Clear.

Background tints for state chips: Clear #E6F2EC · Action needed #FDF0DD · Not assessed #F1F2F4 · Blocked #FBE8E7.

Not assessed is never dimmed into invisibility. It carries the same type size and weight as the other three; grey is its colour, not a de-emphasis. Counts of not-assessed items appear wherever counts of findings appear.

3 · Typography

Instrument Sans · display
Instrument Sans · body. One family across product, marketing and exports, differentiated by weight and tracking only. Tight negative tracking at display sizes, normal tracking below 16px. Tabular figures in every table.
IBM Plex Mono · Reg. (EC) 767/2009 · Art. 15 · ruleset 2026.08 · hash 8f2c…d41
Display36–48px · 600 · −0.045emMarketing heroes, section openers
Title20–24px · 600 · −0.035emScreen titles, report titles
Heading15–16px · 600 · −0.02emPanel and card headers
Body13–14px · 400Interface text, table cells, prose
Mono10–12px · 400 · 0.08em capsArticle numbers, versions, hashes, provenance

IBM Plex Mono is the machine layer and does not vary: anything a machine produced or a regulation named is set in mono. That separation is what makes provenance legible without extra badges. Printed documents run at 12pt minimum; nothing on screen falls below 11px, and no interactive target below 44px on mobile.

Rules. The wordmark is always lowercase and never letterspaced. The square is a full stop, not a bullet: it sits on the baseline at cap-height × 0.28 and is only ever cobalt, ink, or paper reversed. Minimum clear space on all sides equals the height of the square × 4. Minimum wordmark width 72px on screen, 20mm in print.

The .ai suffix stays in the domain, not the mark — carrying it in the logo dates the brand and complicates the later move into human food. The one exception is the marketing site header, where the domain is doing navigational work.

Focus is always a 2px cobalt outline at 2px offset, never a browser default. Hover uses cobalt 600, pressed uses cobalt 700. Disabled controls drop to 45% opacity and lose the accent entirely.

6 · The discipline this direction requires

Cobalt sits close to the blue that interfaces conventionally use to mean "information" or "in progress". Three rules keep that from eroding the verdict vocabulary, and they need to be enforced in review rather than left to judgement:

No cobalt on a results surface. On any screen or document that reports a verdict, cobalt appears only on controls the user can press — never in a row, a cell, a badge or a chart.
No blue fifth state. "In progress", "queued" and "checking" are rendered in neutral with mono labels, not in cobalt.
Provenance stays typographic. Rule, model judgement and not assessed are distinguished by mono labelling and glyph, never by tinting them blue.

If any of these three becomes inconvenient in practice, the correct response is to revisit the accent, not to relax the rule. 2a Prussian is the fallback: identical structure, accent dark enough that the conflict cannot arise.

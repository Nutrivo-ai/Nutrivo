A translated legal term is still a defect

On-pack text is legal text, and in most of our markets it has to be legal text in the local language. So we do not translate it. Prescribed designations and mandatory particulars are looked up from the rulebook for that market and language, cited to the article they come from.

Then the artwork is measured again. German runs about thirty per cent longer than English, and a particular that was compliant can breach a minimum character height or leave the panel entirely. The geometry pass runs per language, on the rendered file.

Label-ready is not the same as sellable

A label can be fully compliant and the product still cannot be sold, because it is not registered in that jurisdiction. Registration is its own state on the board, so a product clear on every label check but unregistered in eleven states reads as not ready — with the eleven named.

What we will not do

We name every registration you owe — the authority, the route, the evidence, the fee, the deadline — and pre-fill everything your product record already answers. We do not submit it, pay it, or correspond with the authority. Only the operator named on your label can do that, and any tool that implies otherwise is selling you a risk.

Priced on what you manage, never on what you run

Checks and re-checks are unlimited on every tier, including free. Metering them would charge you for the one habit that makes labels safer. Language-correct checking is never a tier either: if you are subscribed to a market, we check in that market’s language.
